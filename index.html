Allowances

Your spouse or civil partner transfers £1,260 of their Personal Allowance to you, cutting your tax by up to £252.

Child Benefit

If your adjusted net income is over £60,000, you repay some Child Benefit through the High Income Child Benefit Charge: 1% for every £200 over, so all of it at £80,000. Adjusted net income is your total taxable income less pension contributions. The calculator assumes you are the higher earner in your household, who pays the charge.

Your tax in 2026-27

Employers pay National Insurance of 15% on your pay above £5,000. Your employer hands it over, but most of the cost falls on you, through lower pay than you would otherwise get. The calculator counts 75% of it as your tax, close to the Office for Budget Responsibility's estimate that 76% of the 2025 rise passes to workers.

    Taxes are not earmarked for particular services. The government pools what it raises and decides spending separately. In 2025-26 it also borrowed to cover part of what it spent, so taxes paid for less than all of it. The chart shares your tax across spending in the same proportions as the Treasury's total.

    Outturn means what was actually spent in a year that has ended, as opposed to plans.

    The figures are the Treasury's total expenditure on services: £1,227.6 billion in 2025-26. That is spending on public services, benefits and debt interest. It leaves out £132.5 billion of accounting adjustments, mostly depreciation (the wearing out of roads, buildings and equipment) and VAT that public bodies pay and get back, which take the total to £1,360.1 billion. HMRC's Annual Tax Summaries use the same basis.

    Spending is grouped by purpose using the UN's COFOG classification (Classification of the Functions of Government), whichever department or council does the spending. The methodology explains each category.
